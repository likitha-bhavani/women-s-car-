PCOS Treatment Guide

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting many women. Treatment is highly individual and may include a combination of medical, lifestyle, and supportive therapies. This guide summarizes the main options to help you and your healthcare provider create a plan that works for you.

Medical Treatments

  • Medications for Menstrual Regulation: Birth control pills, progestin therapy
  • Ovulation Induction: Clomiphene, letrozole, metformin (for fertility)
  • Anti-Androgen Medications: Spironolactone, flutamide (for excess hair/acne)
  • Insulin-Sensitizing Agents: Metformin
  • Other: Eflornithine cream for facial hair

Lifestyle Changes

  • Diet: Focus on high-fiber, low-GI foods, lean proteins, and healthy fats. See our PCOS Diet Guide.
  • Exercise: Regular physical activity (aerobic, strength, yoga) helps with weight management and hormone balance.
  • Weight Management: Even modest weight loss can improve symptoms and fertility.
  • Sleep: Aim for 7-9 hours of quality sleep per night.

Alternative & Supportive Therapies

  • Supplements: Inositol, vitamin D, omega-3 (consult your doctor first)
  • Acupuncture: May help with hormone balance and stress
  • Stress Management: Meditation, mindfulness, therapy (see resources)

Mental Health Support

PCOS can impact emotional well-being. Seek support from a counselor, support group, or mental health professional if needed. See our Mental Health Resources.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

  • Can PCOS be cured? There is no cure, but symptoms can be managed with the right treatment plan.
  • Can I get pregnant with PCOS? Many women with PCOS can conceive with the right treatment and support.
  • Is weight loss necessary? Not always, but it can help improve symptoms for many women.
  • Should I take supplements? Only under the guidance of your healthcare provider.
