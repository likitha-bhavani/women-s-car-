PCOS Diet Guide

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder that can be managed with the right nutrition and lifestyle. This guide provides evidence-based dietary recommendations to help you manage PCOS symptoms and improve your well-being.

Recommended Foods

  • High-Fiber Foods: Whole grains, oats, brown rice, quinoa, lentils, beans, fruits, and vegetables.
  • Lean Proteins: Chicken, fish, tofu, eggs, Greek yogurt, legumes.
  • Anti-Inflammatory Foods: Berries, leafy greens, tomatoes, olive oil, nuts, turmeric, ginger.
  • Healthy Fats: Avocado, nuts, seeds, fatty fish (salmon, sardines).
  • Low-Glycemic Index Foods: Sweet potatoes, non-starchy vegetables, most fruits.

Foods to Avoid

  • Processed sugars and sweets (cakes, candies, sugary drinks)
  • Refined carbohydrates (white bread, white rice, pastries)
  • Fried and fast foods
  • Excessive dairy and red meat (for some women)
  • High-sodium packaged foods

Sample PCOS-Friendly Meal Plan

Breakfast: Oatmeal with berries and chia seeds
Snack: Apple slices with almond butter
Lunch: Grilled chicken salad with mixed greens, tomatoes, and olive oil dressing
Snack: Greek yogurt with walnuts
Dinner: Baked salmon, quinoa, and steamed broccoli

Nutrition Tips

Eat regular, balanced meals to help stabilize blood sugar levels.
Stay hydrated and limit caffeine and alcohol.
Consider supplements like inositol, vitamin D, and omega-3 (consult your doctor first).
